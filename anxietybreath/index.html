AnxietyBreath — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 October 2026

AnxietyBreath ("the app", "we") is a guided-breathing and mood-journal app that keeps your journal on your device. This policy says exactly what the app does and does not do with your data.

The short version

  • Your journal, your mood ratings and your breathing history never leave your phone. Everything stays in app-private storage on your device and is never uploaded to us or to anyone else.
  • No account. No login, no email, no profile.
  • We never sell or rent your data, and we never share your content with anyone.
  • The app does connect to the internet — not for your content: only for crash reports and anonymous usage counts from Google Firebase. Section "What the app sends" lists every one of them.
  • No ads. The app shows no advertising and does not use the advertising ID.

What is stored on your device only

  • Your journal entries: the date, the 1–5 mood rating, the trigger tags you pick and your note.
  • Your completed breathing sessions (date, pattern and number of cycles), from which the streak is counted.
  • A breathing session you left part-way (its pattern and how far you were), so you can resume it.
  • Your settings, including the daily reminder and its time.

This data lives in local, app-private storage. Android's cloud backup is switched off for this app, so it is not copied to your Google account either. You can edit or delete any journal entry in the app; uninstalling the app permanently deletes everything. We have no copy and cannot recover it.

What the app sends

The release build declares these permissions: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (network access, used only for what the table below lists); POST_NOTIFICATIONS (to show the daily reminder, if you switch it on); RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED (to set your reminder again after the phone restarts); VIBRATE (the optional haptic cues during a session); FOREGROUND_SERVICE and WAKE_LOCK (merged in by the Google Firebase / Android libraries; the app runs no foreground service of its own); com.google.android.c2dm.permission.RECEIVE (merged in by the bundled Firebase Cloud Messaging library, which is switched off — see below); BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE (used by Firebase Analytics to record which store the app was installed from). It does not declare the advertising-ID permission or any exact-alarm permission. Several of these are merged in by the Google libraries the app bundles rather than used by the app itself.

Third party What it receives Why
Google Firebase Crashlytics Crash stack traces, device model, OS version, app version; a Firebase installation ID for this copy of the app and app-session start events (sent to Firebase's logging service) So we can fix crashes
Google Firebase Analytics Event names (app_open, session_completed, mood_logged); mood_logged carries only how many trigger tags you picked — never your mood rating, the tag names or your note; plus what Firebase records automatically for every app: an app-instance ID that resets when you reinstall, device model, OS version, app version and an approximate country derived from your IP address So we can tell which features are used

Your mood ratings, journal notes, trigger tags and session history never appear in any of these. The app uses no remote configuration service and sends no push messages: the Firebase Cloud Messaging library ships inside a shared notifications component but is switched off, so it registers no push token.

Crash reports and analytics are processed by Google on our behalf under Google's privacy policy.

Advertising

The app shows no ads. An advertising library is bundled with the code but is switched off in this release: no ad is requested and no ad is shown. The app's manifest removes the advertising-ID permission (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID) and the Android Ad Services permissions, and Firebase Analytics is configured not to collect the advertising ID. If we ever turn ads on, we will update this policy and the Play Data safety form before the release that does it.

Health

AnxietyBreath is a relaxation aid, not a medical device, and does not diagnose or treat any condition. If you feel unsafe or are in crisis, contact your local emergency services.

Children

AnxietyBreath is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Your rights

Your journal is stored only on your device — edit or delete entries in the app at any time, or uninstall to erase everything. For the crash and analytics data listed above, Google acts as the processor — see Google's privacy policy for access and deletion requests.

Changes

We may update this policy; the "last updated" date will change accordingly.
