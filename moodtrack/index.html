MoodTrack — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 August 2026

MoodTrack ("the app", "we") is built privacy-first: your mood diary stays on your device, encrypted. This policy says exactly what the app does and does not do with your data, including the parts that are less flattering.

The short version

  • Your mood entries never leave your phone. They are stored locally, encrypted at rest on Android (SQLCipher), and are never uploaded to us or to anyone else.
  • We do not require an account. No login, no email, no profile.
  • We never sell or rent your data, and we never share your entries with anyone.
  • The app does connect to the internet. Not for your entries — for crash reports, anonymous usage counts and purchases. Section "What the app sends" lists every one of them.

What is stored on your device only (never transmitted)

  • Your mood ratings, notes and tags — held in encrypted, app-private storage and, if you turn it on, behind an app lock.

How it is stored, precisely. On Android — the version distributed on Google Play — your diary is kept in a SQLite database (moodtrack.db) that is encrypted at rest with SQLCipher. Every database page is encrypted. The key is a per-install random passphrase generated on your device and held in the Android Keystore-backed secure store; it is never transmitted and we never see it. If your device's keystore cannot return the key, the app tells you and offers a retry — it never silently rotates the key, because that would destroy your entries.

The app lock (fingerprint / face / device PIN) is an additional, separate gate on the screen. The encryption above does not depend on it and applies whether or not you enable it.

Uninstalling the app permanently deletes this data. We have no copy and cannot recover it.

A backup you export is a byte-for-byte copy of that same SQLCipher-encrypted database file, so it is still encrypted after it leaves the app. It leaves only through your device's share sheet, to whichever app or person you choose. We never see it. Note that it can only be opened with the key held on the device that produced it.

What the app sends

MoodTrack itself declares one permission: USE_BIOMETRIC, for the app lock (the AndroidX biometric library adds USE_FINGERPRINT alongside it for older Android versions). Everything else in the Play Data safety form comes from the libraries below, which merge the following into the final app: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE; the advertising ID permission com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID; the three Android Ad Services permissions ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS; com.android.vending.BILLING and com.google.android.finsky.permission.BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE for purchases; and WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE — we run no foreground service of our own. Here is each one and what it is for:

Third party What it receives Why
Google Firebase Crashlytics Crash stack traces, device model, OS version So we can fix crashes
Google Firebase Analytics Event names only — e.g. screen_opened, paywall_shown — with no parameters attached So we can tell which screens are used
Google Play Billing Your purchase So PRO can be sold
RevenueCat Your purchase / entitlement status (e.g. "premium active") and an anonymous app user ID So PRO follows you to a new device
Google Mobile Ads SDK Advertising ID availability The SDK is bundled; ads are switched off in this release and no ad is requested or shown

No rating, journal text, tag, date or entry id ever appears in any of these. Our analytics events carry no parameters at all, and an automated test in our build (AnalyticsSafetyTest) fails the build if a parameter is ever added. No log line in the app prints your entries either.

We never receive your payment card details; those are handled solely by Google.

What the app never touches

No camera. No microphone. No location. No contacts. No access to any other app's data.

Advertising

The Google Mobile Ads SDK ships inside the app but advertising is switched off in this release, so no ad is requested and no ad is shown. Because the SDK is present, Android still lists the advertising-ID permission for the app. If we ever turn ads on, we will update this policy and the Play Data safety form before the release that does it.

Children

MoodTrack is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Your rights

Your entries are stored only on your device — edit or delete them in-app at any time, or uninstall to erase everything. For the crash and analytics data listed above, Google acts as the processor — see Google's privacy policy for access and deletion requests.
